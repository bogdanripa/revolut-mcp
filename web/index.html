Ask your AI about your business bank account.

Revolut MCP connects Claude, ChatGPT or any MCP client to your Revolut Business account — balances, transactions, counterparties and payments, in plain language.

What you can ask it to do

See where the money is

Every account with its balance and currency, plus the IBAN, BIC, sort code or routing number for any of them.

Go through the statement

Transactions over any date range or account, with counterparty, amount, state and fees — the raw material for reconciliation.

Reconcile against invoicing

Pair it with an invoicing MCP server and ask which invoices actually got paid, and which are still outstanding.

Manage counterparties

List, inspect, add and remove the people and companies you pay, by IBAN/SWIFT or local account details.

Move money — if you allow it

Payments between your own accounts and out to counterparties, plus currency exchange at Revolut's rate. Only if you grant the permission.

Know your team

Who is on the business account and what role they hold.

Connect it in three steps

  1. Copy the connector URL.
    …
  2. Add it to your assistant.

    In Claude: Settings → Connectors → Add custom connector, paste the URL, save. Leave any token or client-ID fields empty — the connection is set up in the next step.

  3. Register the certificate and approve in Revolut.

    Your assistant opens our connect page. It hands you a certificate and a redirect URI to paste into Revolut → Settings → APIs, you paste the Client ID Revolut gives you back, and Revolut asks you to approve. About a minute, once. See it step by step →

Why is there a certificate step at all? Revolut Business has no way to register an API client programmatically — every business creates its own, in its own portal. That one paste is the only manual part; everything after it is driven for you.

Where your credentials live

You approve the connection on Revolut's own site, so your Revolut password and 2FA never touch this server. What we keep is the access token Revolut issues for your business, encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM. Your assistant never receives it — it gets a separate token that maps to your account and can be revoked without touching anything on the Revolut side.

This is a real bank account. Revolut asks which permissions to grant when you approve, and you can grant read-only. Payment tools are exposed, but they only work if you ticked the payment permissions — and every transfer they make is real and irreversible. Grant the least you need.

Prefer to run it yourself?

It also runs over stdio, single-account, on your own machine — no server, no database, tokens in a local file. And the hosted deployment is one container plus a Postgres database, so you can stand up your own copy from the same repository.
